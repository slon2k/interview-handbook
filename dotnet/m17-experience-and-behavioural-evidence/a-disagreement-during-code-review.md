# A Disagreement During Code Review

## What Interviewers Are Listening For

Whether you can disagree on substance without becoming defensive or dismissive, on either side of the disagreement. This connects directly to Module 15's code-review content — the same collaborative, non-adversarial posture, now tested through a real remembered conflict rather than an abstract principle.

## Prompts to Find Your Real Story

- Did you push back on a reviewer's suggestion because you believed your approach was actually correct — and were you right, or did the discussion change your mind?
- Did you, as a reviewer, flag a design or correctness concern (Module 4/14) that the author initially disagreed with?
- Was there a disagreement about a trade-off with no objectively "correct" answer — e.g., whether a repository abstraction was worth its cost (Module 4/10/14), or whether a piece of logic belonged in the application layer vs. the domain (Module 14)?
- Was there a disagreement that was really about unstated assumptions or missing context, resolved once that context surfaced?

## A Generic Shape (Template Only — Fill In Your Actual Details)

```
Situation: [what the disagreement was actually about — be specific about the technical
            substance, not just "we disagreed on the approach"]
Task:      [your position, and why you held it]
Action:    [how the disagreement was actually resolved — did you present evidence, run a
            quick experiment, escalate to a third opinion, or genuinely change your mind
            after hearing the other side?]
Result:    [what was actually decided, and — importantly — how the relationship/working
            dynamic was afterward, since that's often the real signal being tested]
```

## Common Weak Answers

- A story where you were simply right and the other person simply capitulated, with no genuine tension or learning on either side — often reads as one-sided or incomplete.
- Framing disagreement as inherently uncomfortable or something to avoid, rather than a normal, healthy part of review (Module 15).
- No specific technical substance to the disagreement — "we disagreed on style" is much weaker than a concrete design or correctness question.
- Not addressing what happened to the working relationship afterward, leaving the interviewer to wonder if there's lingering friction.

## Follow-up Questions to Expect

- "Did you end up agreeing, or did someone just decide?"
- "How did you make sure the discussion stayed productive rather than personal?"
- "Would you handle it the same way again?"
- "Has your relationship with that person been fine since?"
