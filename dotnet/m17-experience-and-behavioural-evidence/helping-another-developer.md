# Helping Another Developer

## What Interviewers Are Listening For

Whether you can explain something clearly to someone with less context than you, and whether you actually helped them understand it (versus just doing it for them). This is a proxy for how you'd mentor or pair with a teammate on a real team, which matters more the more senior the role becomes.

## Prompts to Find Your Real Story

- Did you help a less experienced teammate debug something — and did you walk them through your reasoning, or just hand them the answer?
- Did you explain a concept from this handbook's territory to someone — async/await's mental model (Module 6), why N+1 happens (Module 10), a security concept (Module 12) — in a way that actually landed?
- Did you pair-program through a hard problem with someone, and what made that collaboration work (or initially not work)?
- Was there a time you realized you were about to just "do it for them" and deliberately chose a slower, more teaching-oriented approach instead?

## A Generic Shape (Template Only — Fill In Your Actual Details)

```
Situation: [what the other person was stuck on, specifically]
Task:      [what you decided your role should be — explain, pair, or just unblock them
            quickly, and why you chose that]
Action:    [HOW you helped — did you ask them questions to guide their own thinking, use
            an analogy, walk through a specific example together?]
Result:    [whether they were able to handle a similar situation independently afterward —
            this is the real signal of whether you actually taught something versus just
            solved it for them]
```

## Common Weak Answers

- A story where you simply solved the problem for them, with no actual explanation or teaching involved — this doesn't demonstrate the mentoring/communication skill being tested.
- No evidence that the help actually landed — if you don't know whether they could handle something similar afterward, that's worth being honest about.
- Choosing an example where the "help" was really just answering a quick factual question, rather than genuinely working through a harder concept together.
- Framing the story in a way that condescends to the person you helped, even subtly, which is itself a signal worth being careful about.

## Follow-up Questions to Expect

- "How did you know they actually understood, rather than just nodding along?"
- "Did they come back with a similar question later, or were they able to handle it themselves?"
- "How do you decide when to explain something versus just doing it quickly yourself?"

## References

This scenario is less mapped to one technical module and more about communication — any concept from Modules 2-14 you've genuinely had to explain to someone else is a strong candidate for the technical substance of this story.
